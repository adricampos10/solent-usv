The guidance law, interactive

Drag the sliders to see how the boat in solent-usv works out its heading to hold a track across a tidal stream. The full explanation is in the README section The physics behind the navigation. Not for navigation.

The whole guidance law at the boat

heading = track angle − β − θ, with β = arcsin(v⊥ / U) into the current and θ = arctan(e / Δ) back to the line. Track 090°, boat below the line, stream setting south. Distances not to scale; angles are.

atan2: the right angle all the way round

The direction from A to B as a maths angle (0° = east, counter-clockwise). Plain arctan(Δy / Δx) loses the signs in the division, so it can't tell west from east.

arcsin: the course to steer

You know the hypotenuse (boat speed U) and the side to cancel (cross current v⊥): sin β = v⊥ / U. It's the tidal vector triangle from passage planning.

arctan: back to the track (line-of-sight guidance)

The boat aims at a point on the track Δ ahead. The legs of the triangle are Δ and e, so tan θ = e / Δ. The dashed red arrow is a linear rule θ = k · e with the same slope near the track (k = 1/Δ rad per metre): it agrees close in, but past 90° it turns the boat back.
